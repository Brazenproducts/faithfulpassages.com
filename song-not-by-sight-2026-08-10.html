The Scripture Behind This Song

2 Corinthians 5:7

"For we walk by faith, not by sight."

Eight words that describe the shape of the entire Christian life. Not a command to ignore reality but a statement of what determines the direction: faith in what is true about God, not the visible circumstances of the moment.

Hebrews 11:8

"By faith Abraham, when called to go to a place he would later receive as his inheritance, obeyed and went, even though he did not know where he was going."

The original "not by sight" moment. Abraham left. He did not have a map, a destination confirmed, or a clear outcome. He had a call and a character — the faithfulness of the one calling him.

Romans 8:24-25

"For in this hope we were saved. But hope that is seen is no hope at all. Who hopes for what they already have? But if we hope for what we do not yet have, we wait for it patiently."

Paul defines hope as inherently future-oriented and unseen. Walking by faith is not a temporary phase until visibility arrives — it is the permanent posture of someone whose ultimate reality is not yet visible.

Going Deeper

The year rarely goes the way we mapped it. Abraham left Ur without a destination. Moses led two million people into a desert without a route. The disciples followed a teacher who kept telling them things they didn't understand until later. The posture of not-by-sight is not a crisis mode. It is the normal mode of following God, most of the time.

What makes it hard is that we are wired for sight. We make plans, we set goals, we draw maps — and those are not bad things. But the moment the map and the road diverge, faith is what determines whether we keep walking or stop. Sight says: this is not what I expected. Faith says: the one I'm following knows where we're going even when I don't.

Hebrews 11 — the famous faith chapter — is a catalog of people who did significant things without being able to see the outcome. They "did not receive the things promised; they only saw them and welcomed them from a distance" (v.13). The faith they exercised was real and honored even though most of them died before the fulfillment arrived. That is what walking by faith looks like in a human lifespan.

This song is for the season when your map is obsolete. When what you prayed for didn't happen, or happened differently, or is still pending in ways you can't see. The invitation is not to pretend you can see more than you do but to keep walking with the one whose vision extends beyond yours. That is not blind faith — it is trust in a specific character, based on specific evidence, moving through specific uncertainty. It is the posture of every person in Hebrews 11.

Frequently Asked Questions

What does "walking by faith not by sight" mean in practice?

It means making decisions based on what you believe to be true about God's character and promises rather than what your current circumstances confirm. It is not ignoring reality — it is refusing to give reality the final vote. Practically: when circumstances say one thing and your knowledge of God's faithfulness says another, you act on the latter. Every time you pray without seeing an immediate answer, every time you choose integrity when dishonesty would benefit you, every time you show up in a dry season — that is walking by faith.

Is faith rational?

Yes. Faith in the biblical sense is not credulity or suspension of reason. It is trust based on evidence — the evidence of God's character as revealed in Scripture and in experience. Hebrews 11:1 calls faith "confidence" and "assurance" — both imply a rational basis. The Christian walks by faith not by sight because the unseen reality (God's character, His promises, His active working) is more reliable than the visible circumstance, not because they abandon evidence-based reasoning.

How do I keep trusting God when things don't go as planned?

Separate your trust from the plan. The plan is yours; God's character is not plan-dependent. When Abraham's path diverged from expectation, the constant was not the route but the God who called him. Practically: grieve the plan that didn't happen (that is honest and necessary), then return to the question of what faithfulness looks like in the actual road you are on — not the one you expected.
