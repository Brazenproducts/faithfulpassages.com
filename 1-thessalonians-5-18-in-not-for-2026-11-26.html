1 Thessalonians 5:18 — He Said 'In,' Not 'For'

Thanksgiving doesn't mean pretending — 'in' every circumstance is not the same as 'for' every circumstance.

"Give thanks in all circumstances; for this is God's will for you in Christ Jesus." — 1 Thessalonians 5:18

▶ Listen in English

It's Thanksgiving Day, and this verse will be quoted more today than any other. Someone will say it at the dinner table. Someone will post it. Someone who lost a child this year, or a marriage, or a job, will hear it and feel the quiet shame of not being able to feel what they're supposed to feel — grateful. So before we let that happen, we need to look at one single word.

The word is 'in.' Not 'for.' Paul writes 'give thanks in all circumstances' — not 'give thanks for all circumstances.' He was in prison when he wrote much of what we now call his prison epistles. He wasn't pretending prison was a gift. He was choosing a posture of trust toward a God he believed was good, even when his circumstances weren't. That is a completely different thing than calling suffering a blessing.

The kind of gratitude this verse calls for doesn't require you to redecorate your pain. You don't have to say 'I'm grateful for the cancer' or 'I'm grateful this year destroyed me.' What you can say — what you're invited to say — is: 'Even in this, I am going to hold on to what I know about who you are.' That is faith. That is Thanksgiving.

If you come to the table today carrying something too heavy to dress up as a blessing, this verse was written for you more than anyone. You don't have to perform gratitude. You just have to show up — in the middle of it all — and tell God the truth. That's what 'in all circumstances' actually looks like.

What People Think It Means

Most people hear this verse as a command to be thankful FOR every circumstance — to call cancer a gift, to frame grief as something to celebrate, to spiritually repackage every hard thing as a blessing. That reading turns genuine pain into something to be ashamed of, and makes gratitude into performance.

What It Actually Means

The word is 'in,' not 'for' — and the difference changes everything. Paul wrote this from prison, not from comfort. He wasn't calling prison good; he was maintaining a posture of trust toward a God he knew was faithful even when circumstances were brutal. Giving thanks 'in' all circumstances means your gratitude is directed at God's character, not at the painful thing itself. The two prepositions lead to completely different spiritual postures.

How to Apply It

If this Thanksgiving is one of the hard ones — marked by an empty seat, an unanswered prayer, or a grief that won't let go — you don't have to pretend. You can say 'this has been a hard year AND I still believe you are good' and have that count as thanksgiving. The 'in' is an invitation to show up honestly, not perform gratefully.

En Español

1 Thessalonians 5:18 — He Said 'In,' Not 'For'

▶ Escuchar en Español

Es el Día de Acción de Gracias, y este versículo será citado hoy más que ningún otro. Alguien lo dirá en la mesa. Alguien lo publicará. Alguien que perdió un hijo este año, o un matrimonio, o un trabajo, lo escuchará y sentirá la silenciosa vergüenza de no poder sentir lo que se supone que debe sentir: gratitud. Así que antes de que eso ocurra, necesitamos mirar una sola palabra.

La palabra es 'en.' No 'por.' Pablo escribe 'dad gracias en toda circunstancia', no 'dad gracias por toda circunstancia.' Estaba en prisión cuando escribió gran parte de lo que hoy llamamos sus epístolas desde la cárcel. No estaba fingiendo que la prisión era un regalo. Estaba eligiendo una postura de confianza hacia un Dios en quien creía que era bueno, aunque sus circunstancias no lo fueran. Eso es algo completamente diferente a llamar bendición al sufrimiento.

El tipo de gratitud que este versículo nos pide no requiere que redecores tu dolor. No tienes que decir 'estoy agradecido por el cáncer' ni 'estoy agradecido porque este año me destruyó.' Lo que puedes decir —lo que se te invita a decir— es: 'Incluso en esto, me aferraré a lo que sé sobre quién eres tú.' Eso es fe. Eso es Acción de Gracias.

Si llegas a la mesa hoy cargando algo demasiado pesado como para disfrazarlo de bendición, este versículo fue escrito para ti más que para nadie. No tienes que actuar gratitud. Solo tienes que presentarte —en medio de todo— y decirle a Dios la verdad. Así es como se ve realmente 'en toda circunstancia.'
